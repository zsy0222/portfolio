![Dreams: another doorway into time and inspiration](/images/dream-time-entry.png)

I have always been deeply interested in dreams.

When I watched *Inception* as a child, I seriously considered a question for the first time: **Is time in a dream really the same as time in reality?**

The film imagines time moving faster in dreams: several hours in a dream might correspond to only a few minutes in reality. Although this is an artistic treatment, some of my own dream experiences have made me feel that the question is worth exploring.

One experience in high school left a particularly strong impression on me.

That morning, I woke once at **6:04**. Still very sleepy, I chose to go back to sleep. At about **6:10**, I woke again.

In just six minutes of real time, I felt as if I had experienced a complete story in a dream. It contained continuous scenes, interactions between people, changes of location, and even a clear progression of events. I could not accurately judge how much time had passed in the dream, but the feeling was strong: the time inside it seemed much longer than those six minutes in reality.

For the first time, I truly realized: **the human brain might create an entirely different experience of time.**

I began to want to record my dreams.

I feel dreams may be one of the most important sources of inspiration. Many strange ideas, inexplicable images, and combinations that would never occur in reality can emerge from the world of dreams. A dream is like a creative experiment the brain conducts freely, without the constraints of reality.

But recording dreams is not easy.

They disappear so readily.

Often, at the moment you wake up, you clearly remember the whole dream: where you were, what happened, who was there, and how you felt. Yet a little distraction is enough. Minutes later, the images seem to have been rapidly erased, leaving only hazy fragments.

So if I really want to record a dream, I think I have to capture those first few seconds after waking.

I need to keep mentally repeating the dream's contents to hold the memory together, then sit up immediately, pick up a device, and give the recording my full attention. So many details—a facial expression, a sentence, a scene transition, or even an indescribable feeling—gradually disappear after waking.

I wonder whether someone will one day truly understand the rate at which time seems to pass in dreams.

If humans could understand or even control dreams, a new way of living might emerge:

Creating, learning, and working in dreams; traveling, exploring, and experiencing life in the real world.

Perhaps a single day in reality could contain more dimensions of lived experience.

Of course, I am still a long way from studying the brain. My major combines software engineering and business, and I know almost nothing about neuroscience or brain science. Even so, I feel that technological development may eventually connect computers and human consciousness, helping us better understand our own minds.

Perhaps one day dreams will no longer be only random fantasies during sleep, but a doorway through which humanity explores its own consciousness.

Until then, I want to begin with something simple:

Recording every dream I wake from.

Because no one knows whether the next dream may contain an idea for the future.
